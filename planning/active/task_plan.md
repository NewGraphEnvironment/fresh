# Task: Rule condition: a river of at least Q m³/s within D m downstream (#259)

**If done:** a habitat rule can admit a segment that fails its size test when a large river lies a short distance downstream, so small streams and wetland reaches beside a river count, and those far from one do not. **If never:** a rule can only test a segment's own size and slope, and a config that wants "beside a river" has to fall back on a free pass (`thresholds: false`) or a long connection distance.

Juvenile chinook use small streams close to large rivers. Tuning chinook rearing against provincial electrofishing records found the best rules all admit a small stream when a river of about 3 m³/s lies within about 1 km downstream. fresh's rule grammar has no way to say that: a rule tests a segment's channel width, discharge and gradient, and `requires_connected` measures distance to spawning, not to a river.

Branch `259-rule-condition-a-river-of-at-least-q-m-s`. Version after: 0.41.0. Consumer: link#364.

## Decided at the gate (operator, 2026-10-20, on the parked plan; alternative named)

1. **Walk on the working table, not province-wide FWA.** Same scope as `requires_connected`; no new discharge plumbing, the bundle's `mad_m3s` is already there. Cost: truncation at the group's outlet, counted and reported per build. Alternative: walk raw FWA with a discharge lookup table passed from the caller (new plumbing in fresh and link).
2. **Targets** = main-flow rows (`blue_line_key = watershed_key`) with `mad_m3s >= discharge_min`, excluding connectors (1400, 1450), whose flow is the tributary's. (A segment whose own `mad_m3s >= Q` is not an origin: decision 7.) Alternative: also exclude 1100/1150/1300/1350/2300 for parity with the knowledge walk (already gone through the main-flow filter).
3. **Bank, not centreline**: distance to the target minus the length of connector rows on the path that carry the target's `waterbody_key`. A river passed through because it is too small keeps its connector in the distance. Known approximations, documented: a connector with NULL localcode is not on the table, so a passed-through polygon's crossing is short by it; a side-channel origin (1100/1150) jumps to the main stem (pre-existing trace behaviour).
4. **Grammar**: `near_river: {discharge_min, distance_max}`, both finite > 0, no other keys; allowed on spawn and rear; refused on `waterbody_type: L/W`. **With no size test in the rule** (`thresholds: false` without `channel_width`/`mad`, or `channel_width` stripped under `mad`) **near_river is the test on its own** — one definition, and it covers the `mad` strip. Alternative: loader error.
5. **Shape**: a persisted sibling `<to>_near_river(id_segment, watershed_group_code, discharge_min, dist_m)`, rebuilt per group on every classify (DELETE by group + INSERT), kept after classify so link's validator can read it; built by an exported `frs_near_river()`. The compiled predicate reads it: `(<size> OR EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM <near> n WHERE n.id_segment = s.id_segment AND n.watershed_group_code = s.watershed_group_code AND n.discharge_min = Q AND n.dist_m <= D))`. `frs_habitat_predicates()` gains `near_river_table = NULL` and errors, naming classify, when a rule carries the key and no table is given.
6. **One walk serves every Q**: capped at `Dmax + Lmax`, where `Lmax = max over waterbody_key of SUM(length_metre) FILTER (edge_type IN (1400, 1450))` on the table (a split connector is several rows; the prefilter radius and the `downstream_capped` cap must both cover the bank subtraction), with per-Q caps applied in the final SELECT and again in the predicate. Explicit `ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW` frames with `id_segment` as the last ORDER key; `waterbody_key IS NULL` rows subtract nothing. Both `discharge_min` literals (stored and compared) go through `.frs_sql_num()`.
7. **Only a small stream walks** (Plan review finding 1; confirm at resume): origins are rows with `mad_m3s IS NULL OR mad_m3s < discharge_min`, and there is no own-row `dist_m = 0` case. Otherwise under `mad` a segment above the species' MAD maximum (CO rear 0.03–40, CH 0.28–100) fails its size test and passes on `near_river` because its first downstream row reads distance 0. A large river relies on its size test, as the issue's "admit a small stream" says. The predicate still carries no `s.mad_m3s` reference, which link's validator relies on (`.lnk_hv_relax()` substitutes `s.mad_m3s` / `s.channel_width`). This supersedes "passes at distance 0" in decision 2.
